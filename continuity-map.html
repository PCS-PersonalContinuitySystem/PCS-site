A table for first tries.

Mara had not lost the whole idea when she put away the plan for a shop. Its first page was about helping people leave knowing how to begin. She was already learning to do that with Noor, and had felt the difference herself when Nessa let her sew the pocket.

The missing connection was practical: Jules’s side table, Nessa’s wish to repair her father’s box, borrowed tools and one agreed morning. On 3 October, three people came. Nessa fixed the hinge herself. Noor helped another learner repair a puppet. The third person left with a clear next step.

Liv’s photograph caught Mara with her hands on her knees while Nessa worked. She called it “Your turn.” Mara wrote the date beside the twelve-year-old notebook page. The idea had become something she had actually done—and she still had her Sunday for rowing.
