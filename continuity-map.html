One life, overlapping projects.

Mara maintains buses at Northbank depot. She and her partner Sam moved into an old house near the river in May; their daughter Liv is fifteen. These six weeks follow a leaking roof, a possible change at work, a school photography project, visits with her father Pavel, and the small plans competing for the same weekends.

A work question can connect with a family afternoon. An old photograph can change a house project. These notes include decisions that are still open, guesses that needed correcting, and small details that would be easy to lose in a tidy summary.

The record is the evidence.

Each theme has a count of its supporting records. A connection counts records shared by two themes. Select it to read those records and any authored connection note. The website sample uses explicit theme tags to make those counts inspectable; PCS derives its Map from saved memory and notebook wording and topic labels.

Solid and dotted lines make dense views easier to read. The 60% dotted target is a presentation choice, not a confidence score. Equal-count links stay together, so the actual percentage can differ. The 2+ filter is separate: it keeps only connections supported by more than one record.

The app Map is a bounded view of saved content. A missing line does not prove that two parts of your life are unrelated, and a visible line does not establish cause or hidden beliefs.

Give the question a different view.

  • Focus rings: place one theme at the center and inspect its neighborhood.
  • Compare: choose two themes and see their shared and separate surroundings.
  • Grid: spread themes into a regular arrangement for scanning.
  • Saved-date: arrange themes by their latest supporting saved date. These are record dates, not dates of life events.
  • List: browse with ordinary buttons when a graph is not the best way to read.

Search for a theme, filter memories or notebooks, drag nodes, zoom, hide a distracting item, or go back through your selections. Reset the view to begin again. Website changes last only while this page is open.

Take a useful thread back to Conversation.

In PCS you can save Map views, write connection notes, arrange or hide items, and inspect the supporting records. Review the app’s draft before placing it in Conversation. Browsing the Map does not require a running model; using evidence in an AI conversation follows your sharing choices.

The website is a focused demonstration. It does not open a vault, edit PCS records, persist your view, or call a model.

While making soup, Sam moved the chopping board from the uneven section of worktop to the washing-machine lid for the third time. That was the kitchen problem Sam most wanted solved. Mara had been comparing complete sets of units, partly because it seemed wasteful to improve something they might later replace. Over dinner they drew a smaller plan on an envelope: one level preparation surface, a shelf for the heavy pans, and room to open the back door without moving a chair. The roof still came first. A modest kitchen job after that would not settle the eventual renovation, but it could make the room easier to use this autumn.

Original passage

Sam doesn’t mean “new kitchen” in the same way I do. He means somewhere flat to cut an onion and not having the biggest pan fall on him. We drew the actual problems on an envelope over soup. It is a much shorter list than the catalogue.

Liv wanted the camera, not an itinerary

Mara offered Liv a Sunday walk by the river and started describing a route before Liv had answered. Liv declined the organised outing but later asked to borrow the old camera for the footbridge. Mara clarified after another conversation that Liv had not lost interest in the river or in taking pictures. She wanted to decide where to stop and sometimes go with a friend. The distinction mattered because Mara had nearly packed the camera away again, assuming the hobby was hers alone. They agreed that borrowing it did not require joining a family walk. Mara could still take her own photographs on a different stretch of the towpath.

23 August: Liv said she likes the river. She did not want me planning her whole afternoon. Don’t turn her refusal of my suggested outing into “Liv dislikes river walks” or “photography is our shared activity.” She gets to choose.

Original passage

Liv said the river walk sounded boring, then asked where my old camera was. I don’t quite understand that. I thought we could make a proper afternoon of it, bridge and towpath and a stop for chips, but apparently that was too much planning.

Two hours at the repair café

2026-08-22 · memory · Jules / Repair café / Weekend time

Jules asked whether Mara could cover a full Saturday at the repair café because two regular volunteers were away. Mara offered the first two hours instead. She wanted to help, but the house already had an unfinished list and Sam was working at the library until lunchtime. Jules accepted the shorter slot without trying to extend it. They repaired a desk fan together and spent nearly as long finding the correct box for its spare screws. Mara enjoyed the work more once she knew when she would leave. She did not want a standing promise to cover every absence, though she was happy to be asked about particular dates.

Original passage

I told Jules I could do ten until twelve, not the whole day. She just said that would help and wrote it down. Somehow I’d prepared for a negotiation that didn’t happen. Came home with enough afternoon left to do nothing useful for half an hour.

19 August: “Noor’s bus fault” meant the job assigned to her. She did not cause it. The old repair predates her placement, and her observations helped us find it. Keep the missing handover history separate from her work.

A bowl under the dining-room light

2026-08-17 · memory · The old house / Roof and rain / Household budget

Rain came through beside the dining-room light before Mara’s early shift. She put a mixing bowl underneath and moved the table, then spent the bus ride to work thinking about the kitchen units they had been pricing. The house fund held £1,800. She did not yet know whether the problem was a slipped tile or a much larger repair, so she wanted a roof inspection before committing any of that money to the kitchen. The house had been theirs since May, but this was the first problem that made the order of work feel less like a choice. The dining room remained usable once the rain stopped.

Original passage

There’s a bowl on the dining-room table and it is not for breakfast. Rain is coming in beside the light. We have £1,800 in the house pot, so the kitchen browsing needs to pause until someone has actually looked at the roof.
